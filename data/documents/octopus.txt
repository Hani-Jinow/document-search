Octopuses are among the most intelligent invertebrates on Earth. One of their most unusual biological features is that they have three hearts. Two of these hearts pump blood to the gills, where oxygen is absorbed from the water. The third heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body.

An interesting fact about octopuses is that the heart responsible for supplying the body temporarily stops beating when the animal swims. This is one reason octopuses often prefer crawling along the seafloor rather than swimming for long periods, as swimming is energetically expensive.

Unlike humans, octopuses have blue blood. Their blood contains a copper-based protein called hemocyanin, which transports oxygen more effectively in cold, low-oxygen marine environments. Human blood uses iron-based hemoglobin instead.

Octopuses are also known for their intelligence. They can solve puzzles, open containers, navigate mazes, and learn from observation. Scientists believe their advanced nervous system contributes to these abilities. Remarkably, many of an octopus's neurons are located in its arms, allowing each arm to perform complex movements with a degree of independence.

These unique biological adaptations make octopuses one of the most fascinating animals in the ocean.
